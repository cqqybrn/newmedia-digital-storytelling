## Slide 21

- Forensic Architecture
- Looking at Architecture: Facades, internal walls, floors, etc.
- Expose the structures, systems, and infrastructure that run through them.

- Airport: Separate inbound and outbound corridors, splintering infrastructural ductwork, multiple passport control points, and security checkpoints.

## Slide 23

- INTENTIONALITY OF DESIGN
- Airports utilize a combination of architecture, operational procedures, biometric technologies, and international legal frameworks to control borders.

## Slide 24

- Directional signage
- We walk into a terminal and look for the signs.
- Where's security? Where's the bathroom? Where are the gates?
- Fonts, colors, exact height at which signs are displayed, etc., are all optimized through research.
- 75% of all airports use three typefaces: Helvetica, Frutiger, and Clearview (sans serif) because it’s easier to read at a distance.
- Every inch of letter height adds 40 feet of viewing distance.
- Travelers respond to a subconscious cue like the change of shape from one sign system to another.

## Slide 30

- Airport Spatial Architecture
- Airport design around deterrence, psychological manipulation, and spatial control, rather than direct physical intimidation or scaring.
- Hostile architecture: Panopticon-style spatial visibility and psychological friction.
- Heighten anxiety in individuals.

## Slide 31

- The Airport as a Borderland (Non-Places)
- Critical geographers like Marc Augé describe transit zones as "non-places" where normal legal protections do not apply.
- Airports are designed to keep incoming passengers in a suspended legal state until they pass primary immigration.

## Slide 32

- Panoptic Exposure and "Spatial Terror"
- The Mechanism: High ceilings, expansive glass curtain walls, elevated security catwalks…NO PLACE TO RETREAT.
- Open sightlines, elevated surveillance hubs, bright directional lighting, and long, linear glass corridors remove dead spots.
- This design creates a feeling of continuous observation, increasing cognitive load and micro-anxieties for anyone trying to evade detection.
- The Affective Impact: Leverages spatial exposure to induce hyper-vigilance.
- For individuals traveling under false pretenses or undocumented status, the structural absence of cover triggers an involuntary physiological stress response (elevated cortisol, micro-tremors, increased heart rate).

## Slide 33

- Spatial Asymmetry and Disorientation
- The Mechanism: Asymmetrical spatial access.
- Border officials operate behind elevated pods, tinted glass, or high control desks while travelers are forced into narrow, floor-marked waiting queues.
- The Affective Impact: The layout emphasizes total institutional power over the body.
- STRUCTURAL INEQUALITY: The traveler is placed in a passive, highly visible position, while the agent remains protected and dominant.

## Slide 34

- Chokepoints and One-Way Flow (Funneling)
- The Mechanism: Architectural choke point.
- Automated eGates, high-walled corridors, turnstiles, and non-reversible doors prevent backtracking.
- The Affective Impact: Channeling creates a sense of spatial entrapment.
- The physical architecture makes retreat impossible, heightening psychological pressure as they near primary inspection points.

## Slide 35

- The Architecture of Invisibility (Detention Spatialization)
- * The Mechanism: The deliberate physical isolation of secondary processing rooms, holding cells, and charter flight gates away from the primary passenger flow.
- The Affective Impact: By rendering detention and deportation spaces invisible to standard travelers while keeping them immediately adjacent.
- The terminal operates as a dual space: an efficient lounge for privileged citizens, and an enclosed container for unauthorized migrants.
- The psychological threat of being "pulled out of line" into these unseen spaces acts as a powerful passive deterrent.

## Slide 36

- Spatial Architecture & Physical Deterrence
- Spatial funneling: Spatial Bottlenecks and Channelization.
- One-way spatial flows, glass partitions, and automated border control (ABC) eGates restrict movement into funnel points.
- These force individuals into direct contact with biometric scanners and border enforcement personnel, removing opportunities for evasive behavior.
- Transit zones.
- Holding areas: Manage non-citizens and prevent asylum seekers from setting foot on national soil.
- Benches are not comfortable: induce stress in unauthorized travelers.

## Slide 37

- Relevant Theoretical Concepts
- Affective Design: The practice of shaping built environments to elicit specific emotional states (e.g., submission, compliance, or anxiety) without explicit verbal instruction.
- Defensible Space & Hostile Architecture: Urban design techniques originally meant to deter crime (like bollards, limited access points, and forced lines of sight) adapted for international border enforcement.
- Agamben's "Zone of Exception": The architectural translation of state sovereignty, where transit terminals act as physical spaces detached from local legal protections, leaving the undocumented individual spatially exposed to total state control.

## Slide 38

- Inside Passport Control: Primary and Secondary Inspections
- Objective: Quickly confirm identity, check travel credentials, and determine whether the traveler can enter without further evaluation.
- Average Duration: 30 seconds to 3 minutes per passenger.

## Slide 39

- Process & Workflow
- Document Verification: The officer scans your passport or travel document to check for authenticity and expiration dates.
- Biometric Capture: Depending on the port of entry, this includes digital fingerprinting, facial recognition scanning, or iris scans.
- Database Checks: Automated checks query law enforcement databases (such as INTERPOL, regional lookouts, and flight passenger manifests) for active warrants, lost/stolen passport records, or security alerts.
- Brief Interview: The officer asks standard questions regarding travel purpose, duration, accommodations, and item declarations.

## Slide 40

- DOCUMENT STAMP
- Cleared: Passport stamped or biometrics cleared; traveler proceeds to baggage claim/customs.
- Referred: Traveler is directed to Secondary Inspection for further review.

## Slide 41

- Secondary Inspection
- Specialized, detailed examination conducted when primary officers require more time or information to verify eligibility. Being referred does not mean you are under arrest or guilty of a crime; standard referrals occur routinely for administrative reasons.
- Objective: Resolve discrepancies, verify visa eligibility, complete detailed customs audits, or address flagged database records.
- Average Duration: 15 minutes to several hours, depending on administrative backlog and complexity.

## Slide 42

- OUTCOMES
- Admitted: Discrepancy resolved; traveler enters the country.
- Withdrawal of Application for Admission: Traveler agrees to voluntarily return to their origin point without a formal removal order.
- Expedited Removal / Refusal of Entry: Formal denial of entry, leading to detention until return transportation is arranged, often accompanied by a temporary or permanent bar on future entry.

## Slide 43

- The Principle of Non-Refoulement
- This a core pillar of international refugee law and customary international law.
- Prohibits states from transferring, expelling, deporting, or returning (refouler) any individual to a territory where they would face a real risk of persecution, torture, or serious harm.

## Slide 44

- Secondary Inspection
- Officers conduct a specialized assessment (often a Credible Fear or Reasonable Fear interview depending on the jurisdiction) or refer the traveler to a dedicated national protection authority.
- Under non-refoulement, even if a traveler lacks a valid visa, valid passport, or proper entry documentation, they cannot be summarily deported back to a country where their life, liberty, or physical integrity is in danger.
